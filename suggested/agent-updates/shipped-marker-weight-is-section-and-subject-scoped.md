# Convention: a '(shipped)' marker's weight is section- and subject-scoped — it lends nothing to claims the section does not itself make

**Rule.** The elevated evidentiary weight of a "(shipped)" marker applies only to the individually checkable claims the marked section itself makes about its own subject. It does not radiate outward: a marked section lends no weight to (a) unmarked prose elsewhere in the same doc, or (b) a disputed fact the section does not specifically address. A marked section's silence on a fact is no evidence at all — not weak support, not weak refutation. Conversely, a claim cannot borrow the marker by proximity: tucking an adjacent-subject or aspirational sentence into a marked section to inherit its weight manufactures false evidence exactly as mislabeling a design intention does.

**Grounding.**
- `suggested/workflows/reconcile-grok-model-pick-persistence-drift.md`, recording evidentiary weights at registration: "neither current statement sits in a '(shipped)'-marked section covering Grok's pick specifically" — the presence of the marked "TUI usage (shipped)" table in the same doc was correctly credited with nothing, because its claims are about /runtime, /model, and the codex/claude keys, not the Grok pick.
- `suggested/agent-updates/shipped-marker-doc-sections.md` defines the marker's unit as the section and the claim: "the section is explicitly marked '(shipped)'" and "a claim inside a shipped-marked section is treated as a report of live behavior and outranks..." — the weight attaches to claims inside the section, never to the doc.
- `suggested/workflows/audit-shipped-markers-against-live-behavior.md` audits at the same grain: "For each marked section, extract its individually checkable claims and verify each against the running product."

**Why:** the marker is the doc-side proxy for on-wire evidence, and proxies are only trustworthy at the scope where someone actually asserted "this is what the product does today." The Grok-persistence registration shows the scoping question arises in practice and had to be reasoned out ad hoc; without the rule written down, a future adjudicator could plausibly argue "the doc is a shipped doc" and let one marked table silently outrank correct evidence about a fact it never mentions. That failure is invisible in the row — the quote and marker are both real; only the scope reasoning is wrong.

**How to apply:** when recording a drift row's evidentiary-weight signals, credit a "(shipped)" marker only if the quoted claim sits inside a marked section *and* speaks to the disputed fact's specific subject — record "no marked section covers this fact" explicitly when true, as the Grok-persistence row does. When auditing markers, verify claims the section makes, not claims it merely sits near. When writing or editing docs, keep aspirational or adjacent-subject material out of marked sections — split it into its own unmarked section rather than letting it borrow weight.
