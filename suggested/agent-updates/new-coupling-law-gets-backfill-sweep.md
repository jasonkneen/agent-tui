# Convention: a newly landed coupling law gets a backfill sweep over pre-existing violations

**Rule.** A coupling law ("X lands in the same change as Y") governs only changes made **after** it lands — it is structurally blind to the violations that already exist. Therefore landing such a law includes, in the same change or immediately after, a one-time (and repeatable) **backfill sweep**: enumerate pre-existing violations from present reality, and repair each through the law's own procedure, attributed and dated. A coupling law with no backfill leaves the workspace asserting a rule its own history visibly breaks.

**Why:** this has now happened twice — the supersession-marker law and the registry-registration law each landed, were immediately contradicted by pre-existing state, and needed a retroactive backfill artifact written after the failure was noticed. Per the convergence convention, two independent occurrences of one procedure are the extraction signal. The deeper hazard is an apparent-blessing failure: a live coupling law makes the workspace *look* governed, so readers assume existing pairs comply — precisely when the pre-law pairs are the ones that don't.

**How to apply:** when authoring any convention of the form "A and B change together" (tracking-surface coupling, supersession coupling, registration coupling, asset/license pairing), check present reality for pairs the rule would have coupled. If any exist, author or run the backfill sweep as part of landing the law — enumerating from reality per the audit convention, never from a remembered list, with each repair carrying author and date. If none exist, say so in the law's Grounding so the check is visibly done.
