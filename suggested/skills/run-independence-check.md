# Run the independence check on a convergence claim

The test is stated in its fullest form by the family-cataloging skill — "a reader can open the cited paths and confirm none derives from another. Two artifacts quoting a common ancestor is one instance, not a family" (`suggested/skills/catalog-failure-family.md`, Step 1) — and made "an explicit gate rather than an adverb" by the convention (`suggested/agent-updates/convergence-requires-independence-check.md`). Every rung of the convergence ladder leans on it: law promotion, checklist extraction, genre authoring, family naming. Per the extraction law — two independent restatements of one procedure is the extraction signal (`suggested/agent-updates/two-restatements-trigger-extraction.md`) — this skill is the single procedure the gate cites.

## Step 1: Collect the claim's cited paths

List every path the convergence claim offers as an independent source. An uncited source cannot count — the gate exists so a reader can re-verify, and only cited paths are re-verifiable.

## Step 2: Test every pair for derivation

Open both documents of each pair and look for, in descending strength:

- **Direct quotation** — one quotes the converged content from the other.
- **Source citation** — one cites the other as where the rule/procedure/failure comes from (citing it as a *sibling* or *precedent for a different point* does not count; the workspace's docs cross-cite by design, so read what the citation is doing).
- **Shared distinctive wording** — identical uncommon phrasing of the converged content signals copying even without a citation.

A derived member collapses into its source: the pair counts once.

## Step 3: Trace common ancestors

For each surviving source, check whether it cites a third document for the converged content. Two sources both deriving from one ancestor are "one source wearing two paths" — the ancestor counts once and the descendants not at all. Citation chains are the workspace default, so assume ancestry until the text shows independent rediscovery in the source's own words.

## Step 4: Count survivors against the threshold

Extraction needs two independent sources; law promotion and family naming need the plural independence their claims assert. Outcomes, enumerated:

- **Passes** — survivors meet the threshold; proceed with the promotion, extraction, or catalog.
- **Downgrades** — survivors exist but fall below the claimed strength (e.g. "three artifacts" collapses to two): restate the claim at the strength the evidence supports.
- **Fails** — one survivor. The promotion is speculation; the content stays a per-doc instruction, and the check's result is worth recording where the claim was going to be made, so the next occurrence knows it is the second.

## Step 5: Record the check

The promoting artifact states that the check ran and what each pair showed, so a later reader — or the family-catalog audit re-running independence — can verify it rather than trust the adverb.
